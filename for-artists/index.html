03 What happens next

From your file to a release.

  1. You submit, privately

    Your files go to private storage. They are not published, not indexed, and not attached to any email. You get a confirmation and a submission reference straight away.

  2. The atelier reads it

    A small internal review team looks at the work. Nothing is shown outside that team, and nothing is used for anything other than deciding whether to talk to you.

  3. A conversation, if shortlisted

    If the work is shortlisted we write to you. Shortlisting is a stage of review — it is not an offer and it grants no rights in your design.

  4. A written offer

    If we want to produce it, you receive a written collaboration agreement naming the design, the covered products, the channels, the credit and the royalty. Nothing is made until both parties sign it.

  5. Development and release

    You provide the full, production-ready tech pack and sign the collaboration agreement that make the design eligible for the royalty; then sampling, approvals, production and launch. You are credited, and the royalty is calculated on the net profit from sales of your design.

We do not publish a guaranteed response time. Intake is read in batches by a small team, and we would rather say nothing than promise a date we cannot keep. If a design is shortlisted you will hear from us directly; if you have heard nothing, your submission is still on file and you can ask where it stands at any time.

04 Collaboration terms

Ownership, credit, and a share of the profit.

A sketch is enough to apply. To qualify for the proposed 12% royalty, the selected artist must provide a full, production-ready tech pack and sign a collaboration agreement. The royalty is calculated on the net profit from sales of that artist’s specific design.

An example, in plain language

€1,000 in eligible sales, less €400 in eligible costs, gives €600 profit — and a 12% royalty of €72.

Who owns the work

You keep ownership of what you send. Submitting allows one thing: limited internal review by the people deciding whether to make you an offer. It gives ETERNAL VOID no permission to publish, manufacture or sell your work, or to train AI systems on it — that needs a separate signed agreement.

How you are credited

The agreement names the credit you are given: the name you want to be known by and where it appears — the product page and the campaign materials we agree. If you prefer a studio name or a pseudonym, that is the name we use.

Tech-pack eligibility

You can submit without one. To qualify for the proposed 12% royalty, a selected artist provides a full, production-ready tech pack for the design and signs a collaboration agreement. Uploading a tech pack does not guarantee selection, production or payment.

The details

How net profit is calculated

Sales are what customers paid for your design after discounts and refunds, excluding VAT and separately charged shipping. From that we deduct documented manufacturing, printing, packaging and payment-processing costs attributable to the units sold — each cost once, with its invoice or report. What remains is the profit, and the royalty is 12% of it. ETERNAL VOID covers sampling, marketing and general overhead; they are never deducted.

If refunds and costs in a quarter exceed that quarter’s sales, the difference is carried into the next quarter once, and no negative payment is ever raised. Restocks, new colourways and any other use of the design are covered in the agreement or by a written amendment. These are proposed terms; the signed agreement sets the final ones.

Reporting and payment

We propose itemised quarterly statements, paid within 30 days of quarter end. Each statement shows your design’s sales, every deduction with its document, the profit, and the royalty.

Additional development work

The tech pack is the condition for the proposed royalty. Any further work we ask of you after selection — additional artwork, new colourways, substantial revisions — is scoped in writing before it starts, and that written scope or the signed agreement says whether and how it is paid.

Withdrawing a submission

Write to artists@eternalvoid.co with your submission reference and ask us to withdraw it. We mark the submission withdrawn, stop reviewing it, and delete the uploaded files. We keep a minimal record that a submission existed and was withdrawn, and the accepted terms version — that record is what protects both of us.

The 12% rate, the net-profit calculation and its deductions, the tech-pack and agreement conditions, the payment terms and the reporting format are ETERNAL VOID’s proposal and are being reviewed by the owner, by accounting and by Hungarian counsel before intake opens. 06 Questions

Before you send unpublished work.

Do I need a tech pack to submit?

No. A sketch is enough to apply. A full, production-ready tech pack is needed only from a selected artist, together with a signed collaboration agreement, to qualify for the proposed 12% royalty on net profit. You are welcome to include one now; it does not guarantee selection, production or payment.

How is the royalty calculated?

The proposed royalty is 12% of the net profit from sales of your specific design: sales after discounts and refunds, excluding VAT and separately charged shipping, less documented manufacturing, printing, packaging and payment-processing costs for the units sold. For example, €1,000 in eligible sales less €400 in eligible costs gives €600 profit and a €72 royalty. Sampling, marketing and overhead are never deducted.

Does submitting cost anything?

No. There is no submission fee and no fee at any later stage. If anyone asks you to pay to be considered by ETERNAL VOID, it is not us.

Who actually sees my files?

A small internal review team at ETERNAL VOID, and the technical providers that host the storage and send the email. Files sit in private storage and are reached through short-lived authorised links. They are not published, not indexed, not sent as email attachments, and not shared with a manufacturer unless and until you have signed an agreement that names that access.

Can I submit work made with someone else?

Yes, if you have your co-authors’ permission to submit it and to license it, and you tell us who they are. The same applies to any third-party element in the work — a font, a photograph, a sample. If you cannot clear it, leave it out.

What if you make something similar on your own?

ETERNAL VOID designs continuously and independently. Submitting does not stop us from developing our own work, including work that resembles yours. What the intake terms do is keep your submission out of production unless we sign an agreement with you for it.

How long do you keep my files?

Uploaded files are kept while the submission is under review and for a limited retention period after it is closed, then deleted. The exact period is stated in the Submission Privacy Notice. You can ask us to withdraw and delete earlier at any time.

Do I have to be a professional designer?

No. The programme exists for emerging designers and artists. You need to be old enough to enter a contract where you live, and the work needs to be yours.

What if someone else submitted my work?

If you believe work submitted here is yours and was sent by someone else, write to legal@eternalvoid.co. Tell us what the work is and what your claim is. We suspend review of the submission while we look into it.
